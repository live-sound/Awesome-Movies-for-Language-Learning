This is a practical immersion-viewability catalog: **284 actual movies**, with
105 for English, 66 for Spanish, 54 for German, and 59 for French. It focuses
on the experience of learning while watching—not popularity rankings or a
generic difficulty score.

**Snapshot:** July 13, 2026. Streaming catalogs, audio tracks, subtitles, and
closed captions vary by country, profile language, service, and edition. Use
the linked film source to identify the exact movie, then confirm the target
audio and same-language subtitle tracks before renting, buying, or subscribing.

## Legend

- **Level:** CEFR bands describe comfortable viewing with same-language
  subtitles, not the lowest level that can recognize isolated words.
- **Reading support:** The most useful subtitle and on-screen-text strategy for
  that movie. **CC** means closed captions, which may also identify speakers
  and describe important sounds.
- **Listening profile:** Pace, clarity, accents, overlap, specialist vocabulary,
  music, and sound effects that materially change comprehension.
- **Viewing pressure:** **Low** means a missed line is easy to recover from;
  **Medium** means continuity or pace rewards pausing; **High** means dense,
  overlapping, or plot-critical dialogue requires frequent review.
- **Evidence:** **Film source** identifies the production; **Calculated** means
  transcript or corpus analysis; **Human** means a learner or accessibility
  report; **Editorial** means this list's proficiency or viewability judgment.

The CEFR mappings follow the Council of Europe's
[watching TV, film, and video descriptors](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4),
but every movie-level band remains an editorial estimate unless a separate
calculated or human source is linked.

Authentic feature films rarely provide a comfortable A1 experience: even
family films compress a complete story into 80–120 minutes. The A2 and A2–B1
entries here have unusually concrete plots, clear recurring language, strong
visual context, or deliberate delivery. A1 learners should begin with short
scenes, keep same-language subtitles on, and replay before attempting the whole
film without support.

## Availability and subtitle traps

- A platform may offer the movie in one country but not another. Netflix's
  [language-availability guidance](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/101798)
  documents how location, profile language, title, device, and licensing can
  all change the audio and subtitle menu; other services have similar limits.
- A title can have target-language audio without target-language subtitles, or
  subtitles translated from a different dub script.
- **CC** and ordinary subtitles are not interchangeable. CC usually represents
  the spoken track more closely and adds speaker or sound information.
- Older discs, rentals, library editions, and streaming releases can carry
  different cuts and subtitle tracks. Check the exact edition.
- Forced narrative subtitles translate only selected foreign-language lines;
  they are not complete learning subtitles.
- Auto-generated captions are a fallback, not evidence of complete or accurate
  dialogue coverage.
- Dubs are excluded. Each movie is categorized by its original spoken language,
  with genuinely multilingual productions called out in the row.

## Evidence policy

Every row links a direct film reference. Runtime, country, original language,
and production identity come from that source or an additional linked source.
The level, subtitle strategy, listening profile, and pressure rating are
editorial unless **Calculated** or **Human** evidence is explicitly linked.

This catalog does not copy proprietary difficulty scores. Corrections should
